1. Purpose and Scope

This document describes a system that automates job applications for a staffing agency's bench
consultants. Every 4 hours the system finds new job postings that match each consultant, tailors that
consultant's resume for each job, and fills out the application forms on the job portals. A human -- the
consultant -- reviews and clicks the final submit on every application.

The agency owner controls all data from one central point. Consultants run a small application on their
personal machines that does only the browser work. Nothing sensitive lives permanently on consultant
machines.

1.1 Decisions already made

The following decisions were made during design and are final unless the owner changes them:

   · Every application goes out under the consultant's own name, email, and portal accounts. The
      system does the labor; the identity is always the consultant's.

   · The final submit click is made by the consultant, never by the machine. The app fills the form and
      stops at the review screen.

   · Consultants work independently. If one consultant's login expires, their queue waits for them. Jobs
      are never moved to another consultant.

   · Resume tailoring may reword and reorder what is already true. It must never invent skills, tools,
      employers, dates, or accomplishments. This rule is enforced in the central system where
      consultants cannot change it.

   · Answer approval flow: the consultant fills in an unknown answer, their recruiter approves it, then it
      becomes reusable. Answers about salary expectations and work authorization always route to the
      owner for approval.

   · All records are kept indefinitely: every application, the exact resume file submitted, and every
      answer given on every form.

   · For every matched job the system identifies the job poster and obtains their business email and
      phone. If the poster is missing or generic, it finds two active talent-acquisition contacts at that
      company in that work location. Contact data comes from licensed data providers, never from
      scraping LinkedIn directly.

   · Contacts are stored centrally and reused for 90 days before a fresh pull is allowed, to minimize paid
      lookups. Consultants see the contact attached to each job in their own queue; only the owner and
      recruiters can search the full contact store.

2. System Overview

The system has three parts:
  1. Central Hub -- a cloud server owned by the agency. It searches for jobs, tailors resumes, assigns
      work queues, stores all records, and holds the answer bank. It runs the 4-hour cycle.

  2. Web Portal -- a website served by the hub with three access levels: Owner, Recruiter, and
      Consultant. Each level sees only what it is allowed to see.

  3. Consultant Desktop App -- a small program on each consultant's personal machine. It pulls that
      consultant's queue from the hub, opens the job portals in a real browser using the consultant's
      own saved logins, fills the forms, stops at the review screen, and reports everything back to the
      hub.

Data flows one way for sensitive material: the hub sends each consultant only the jobs, resume files, and
approved answers needed for their current queue. The consultant app sends back status reports and
form question-and-answer records. Consultant machines keep no permanent data.

3. Central Hub -- Functional Specification

3.1 Job discovery engine (runs every 4 hours)

   · Reads each consultant's search criteria file: job titles, keywords, locations, work type (contract,
      full-time, part-time, C2C/W2), minimum pay, excluded companies.

   · Pulls new postings from sources that permit it cleanly: the public job feeds of Greenhouse, Lever,
      and Workable company career pages; job alert emails from Indeed and LinkedIn parsed by the
      system; and a paid search feed (such as SerpAPI for Google Jobs) for coverage.

   · Applies a cheap keyword pre-filter first (title and location match) so only plausible postings go to
      the AI matching step. This cuts AI cost by roughly 90 percent.

   · Removes duplicates using a company + title + location fingerprint stored in the database. Only
      postings first seen after the previous run move forward.

   · For each surviving posting, an AI call decides match or no-match against each relevant consultant's
      criteria and classifies the work type. Matched jobs enter that consultant's queue.

3.2 Resume tailoring engine

   · For each matched job, the hub sends the job description plus the consultant's base resume to the
      AI with a locked instruction set: keep the existing format, section order, and voice; reorder and
      reword bullet points to mirror the job description's keywords; surface relevant real experience.

   · Hard rule, enforced in the hub's system prompt and not editable by any consultant or recruiter: the
      AI may rephrase and emphasize what is already true but must never fabricate. A second cheap AI
      check compares the tailored resume against the base resume and flags any claim that has no basis
      in the original. Flagged resumes go to the recruiter for review instead of the queue.
   · Output is a PDF named Company_Title_Date.pdf, stored in the hub and linked to the application
      record. The consultant app downloads it per job; there is no bulk resume download anywhere in
      the system.

3.3 Queue assignment

   · Each consultant has one queue. A queue item contains: the job link, the portal type, the tailored
      resume, and the approved answers likely needed for that portal's form.

   · If one job matches several consultants, it appears in each of their queues -- each applies under
      their own name. The dashboard flags the overlap for visibility.

   · Daily application caps per consultant are set by the owner. The hub stops assigning once the cap is
      reached.

3.4 Answer bank and approval workflow

   · The answer bank stores reusable answers to screening questions, per consultant (for example:
      years of experience with a tool, notice period, willingness to relocate).

   · When the consultant app hits a question it cannot answer, the question is sent to the hub and
      appears on that consultant's dashboard as an unknown.

   · The consultant types the answer. It sits as pending. The recruiter assigned to that consultant
      approves or corrects it. Only then does it enter the answer bank and get used on future
      applications.

   · Exception: any answer touching salary expectations or work authorization routes to the owner for
      approval, regardless of recruiter.

   · No answer is ever auto-approved. A consultant can never approve their own answer.

3.5 Record store

The hub stores, permanently, for every application:
   · The application record: consultant, job, company, portal, date and time, and final status (submitted,
      waiting on consultant, stalled on login, or skipped with reason).
   · The exact resume PDF that was submitted, as a file, linked to the record.
   · Every question the form asked and the exact answer filled in, as a question-and-answer list
      attached to the record.
   · A full audit log: every login, every resume download, every answer approval, every assignment
      change, every token issued or revoked -- with who, what, when, and from which machine.

3.6 Contact discovery engine

Runs immediately after job matching, for every job that enters a queue. It follows a four-step waterfall,
stopping at the first step that produces a usable contact:
1. Read the posting. LinkedIn and Dice postings usually name the poster; Greenhouse and Lever
    postings usually do not. If a personal name is found, carry it to step 2.

2. Check the contact store first. If that person (or, when no poster was named, that company and
    location) already has a contact pulled within the last 90 days, reuse it. Zero cost. This is the default
    path once the store fills up.

3. Enrich through the licensed provider. If the contact is missing or older than 90 days, look the
    person up by name and company through the Apollo API to get business email, then use Lusha or
    SignalHire credits to obtain the phone number. Store the result with source and date.

4. Fallback for empty or generic posters. If no personal name exists (posting says "HR Team" or
    nothing), search the provider for two active talent-acquisition or recruiting people at that company
    in that work location, ranked by seniority match to the role, and enrich both. Both attach to the job.

· Every job in a queue carries its contact(s): name, title, company, business email, phone, and source.
    The consultant sees them on that queue item; the recruiter and owner see them everywhere.

· De-duplication is by person name plus company. One person contacted for five jobs is one record
    linked five times, not five pulls.

· Each contact record has a do-not-contact flag. Anyone who asks not to be contacted is flagged once
    by a recruiter and the system stops attaching them, permanently.

· Hard rule: the system never scrapes LinkedIn profiles itself. All contact data comes through the
    licensed providers' official interfaces. LinkedIn Sales Navigator remains a manual tool for recruiters
    and is not connected to the system, because it has no official interface for this.

4.1 Dashboard views

   · Owner: agency-wide numbers (applications per day and week, per recruiter, per consultant),
      overlap flags, stalled logins across the bench, pending salary and work-authorization approvals, and
      a search box that returns every application ever made for a name or company, with the resume and
      answers attached.

   · Recruiter: the same views limited to their assigned consultants, plus their approval inbox for
      pending answers.

   · Consultant: their own application history, which resume went out for each job, current queue
      status, their unknowns list to fill in, and the contact card (name, title, email, phone) on each job in
      their queue. Every contact view is logged.

5. Consultant Desktop App

5.1 Installation and identity

   · A small desktop program installed once per consultant machine (Windows and Mac).
   · On first run it activates with a one-time token issued by the owner from the portal. The token binds
      to that person and that machine. The owner can revoke it from the dashboard at any time, which
      kills the app instantly.

5.2 Portal logins -- how authentication works

   · The app never holds or transmits any portal password. On first use of each portal (LinkedIn, Dice, a
      Workday company site, and so on) the app opens a real browser window and steps aside. The
      consultant logs in themselves, including any code sent to their phone.

   · The browser's logged-in session is saved in a protected folder on the consultant's machine only --
      the same way a normal browser stays signed in. Later runs reuse the saved session; no password
      entry needed.

   · When a session expires, the app detects it, pauses that portal's queue, notifies the consultant to
      log in again, and reports the stall to the hub so it shows on the recruiter and owner dashboards.

   · Corporate portals such as Workday require one account per company. The consultant creates each
      once; the session is saved the same way. Expect more one-time logins in the first two weeks, then it
      settles.

5.3 The application cycle

  1. Every 4 hours (with a small random offset so activity does not look machine-timed), the app wakes
      and pulls the consultant's queue from the hub.

  2. For each queue item it opens the portal in the real browser with the saved session, navigates to the
      job, and fills every form field it can from the hub-provided data and approved answers. It attaches
      the tailored resume.

  3. Unknown questions are sent to the hub as unknowns and that application is parked until the
      answer is approved.

  4. Completed forms stop at the review screen. The app lists them for the consultant, who reviews and
      clicks submit on each. The app confirms submission and reports it to the hub with the full
      question-and-answer list.

  5. The app deletes local working files after each cycle. Nothing permanent stays on the machine
      except the saved browser sessions and the app itself.

5.4 Behavior limits built into the app

   · Human-paced form filling: realistic typing speed and pauses, one application at a time, never
      parallel.

   · The daily cap set by the owner is enforced locally as well as at the hub.
   · LinkedIn gets the most conservative treatment: lowest volume, human submit always, and the app

      stops touching LinkedIn for the day if the portal shows any bot-check challenge.
6. Security and Misuse Controls

   · Per-person, per-machine tokens, revocable instantly from the owner dashboard.
   · Consultants get read-only access to their own profile and queue. They cannot see other candidates,

      edit criteria, or change answers directly.
   · Resumes are delivered per job, never in bulk. Every delivery is logged with time, person, and

      machine.
   · Two-person rule on every reusable answer: one fills, a different person approves.
   · Daily application caps per consultant, set centrally.
   · All hub data encrypted at rest; all traffic between app, portal, and hub encrypted in transit

      (standard HTTPS/TLS).
   · Hub server hardened basics: firewall allowing only web traffic, automatic security updates, daily

      encrypted backups of the database and resume files kept off the server, and a restore test once a
      month.
   · The AI no-fabrication rule and the second-check comparison live only in hub code; no portal user at
      any level can weaken them.

8.3 Contact-data cost (separate from AI)

Contact discovery spends provider credits, not AI tokens. Phone numbers cost several times more credits
than emails on every provider. The 90-day reuse rule is the main cost control: month one is the
expensive month while the store fills; from month two onward most jobs hit the store and cost nothing.
The developer must pull current credit pricing from the owner's existing Apollo, Lusha, and SignalHire
plans before build and confirm the monthly allowance covers roughly 1,500 to 2,500 new-contact pulls in
month one at 20 consultants, dropping to a few hundred per month after. The hub must track credit
spend per provider on the owner dashboard and stop pulling (reuse-only mode) when a monthly credit
budget set by the owner is reached.

10. Build Plan -- Junior Developer with Claude Code

Claude Code is Anthropic's development tool: it runs in a terminal or editor on the developer's machine,
reads the project, writes the code across files, runs it, and fixes its own errors under the developer's
direction. A junior developer working this way should deliver the pilot version in 11 to 13 weeks. The
steps below are the working instructions to hand them along with this document.

10.1 One-time setup (day one)

  1. Install Git, Node.js 18 or newer (needed to install Claude Code), and Python 3.11 or newer.
  2. Install Claude Code: open a terminal and run npm install -g @anthropic-ai/claude-code -- then

      run claude and sign in.
  3. Subscription: put the developer on a Claude Max plan ($100/month tier) for the build period. It

      covers heavy daily Claude Code use at a flat rate, which is cheaper than pay-per-token at
      build-phase volumes. Drop to a lower plan after launch, when usage becomes occasional
      maintenance.
  4. Create a private GitHub repository named staffing-autopilot with three folders: hub/ (server,
      scheduler, portal back end), portal/ (React front end), and app/ (consultant desktop app), plus
      docs/ containing this specification saved as SPEC.md (a plain-text copy).
  5. Create a file named CLAUDE.md in the repository root. Claude Code reads this file automatically at
      the start of every session. It must contain: the technology stack from Section 9; the rule that portal
      passwords are never stored or transmitted; the no-fabrication rule text for the tailoring prompt; the
      rule that the audit table is append-only; and the instruction to write tests for every feature.

10.2 Working method (every day)

   · Work one phase at a time (Section 10.3). Start each Claude Code session by pointing it at the
      relevant spec section, for example: "Read docs/SPEC.md Sections 3 and 7. Build the database
      models and a FastAPI service in hub/ covering users, assignments, consultant profiles, job postings,
      queue items, applications, answers, device tokens, and the append-only audit table. Include a seed
      script with sample data and tests."

   · Review every change Claude Code proposes before accepting it. Run the tests. Never accept code
      you have not read.

   · Commit to Git after every working feature -- never let more than half a day of work sit
      uncommitted. Work on a branch; merge to main only when tests pass.

   · When stuck for more than 30 minutes, paste the full error into Claude Code and ask it to explain
      the cause before asking for a fix. If a session has gone in circles, start a fresh session and restate the
      goal in one clear paragraph.

   · Never put real candidate data into the system until Phase 8 security review is done. Build and test
      with the seeded fake data.

11. Pilot Rollout Plan
  1. Pick one recruiter and three bench consultants in one job category. Load their profiles, base
      resumes, and criteria.

  2. Run two weeks on Greenhouse and Lever portals only, daily cap of 5 applications per consultant.
  3. Owner and recruiter review every tailored resume and every submitted application in week one.

      Loosen to spot-checks in week two if quality holds.
  4. Success gate before wider rollout: zero fabricated resume content found, at least 90 percent of

      queued applications reaching the review screen without manual fixes, unknowns per consultant
      falling week over week, no portal account warnings, and contact accuracy above 80 percent on a
      recruiter spot-check of 25 pulled contacts (email deliverable, phone reaches the right company).
  5. Then add Dice, raise caps, and onboard the bench in groups of five consultants per week so support
      load stays manageable.

12. Assumptions and Open Items

   · Consultants consent in writing to the agency processing their resumes and application data through
      this system. A one-page consent form should be signed at onboarding; have your attorney review
      it.

   · Automated interaction sits against the terms of service of some portals even with a human submit.
      The design minimizes risk (human pace, human submit, low caps, consultant-owned logins), but a
      portal can still restrict an account. Consultants should be told this plainly in the consent form.

   · Prices in Section 8 are the published rates as of July 2026 and can change; the developer should
      confirm current rates at claude.com/pricing before launch.

   · Contact data is business contact information (work email, work phone, title) used for legitimate
      recruiting outreach. The system stores no personal home details, honors the do-not-contact flag
